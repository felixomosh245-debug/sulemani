# Handling Common Objections

(Fictional, for testing only.)

Always listen to the full objection, acknowledge it, then answer with facts. Never criticise a competitor by name.

## "It is too expensive."

Compare the daily PAYG cost with what the customer already spends. Many households spend KES 50 to 80 a day on paraffin, phone charging and batteries. The Lite 50 costs KES 45 a day and the customer owns it after 12 months. For businesses, ask how much they lose during each power cut, then compare that with the Biz 1000 daily payment.

## "Solar does not work when it is cloudy."

Panels still produce power on cloudy days, though less. The battery stores power from sunnier days. The remote monitoring unit alerts our service team if the battery is regularly running low, and the technician can advise on usage.

## "I will wait for the grid (KPLC)."

Solar works alongside the grid. Customers with a grid connection use Kilele as backup during power cuts, and their monthly electricity bills drop. The customer does not have to choose.

## "A cheaper system is sold in the market."

Ask what the warranty is, who installs it, and who fixes it if it breaks. Kilele offers certified installation, remote monitoring and a service centre within 60 km. Focus on our strengths, not on the other product's weaknesses.

## "What if I cannot pay one month?"

Explain the PAYG rules honestly: the system locks after 3 days of missed payment and unlocks as soon as the customer pays. There is no penalty fee. Customers can also pay ahead when they have extra money.

## "I need to ask my spouse or family."

Respect this. Offer to come back at a time when both can meet, and leave the product leaflet. Book the follow-up visit before you leave.
